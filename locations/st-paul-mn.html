LOCAL MARKET

St. Paul Construction Market

St. Paul is Minnesota's capital and the eastern half of the Twin Cities metro, with about 311,000 residents inside the city limits. Unlike Minneapolis's mid-rise boom, the St. Paul construction market is heavier on infill multi-family, neighborhood-scale apartment redevelopment along University Avenue and the Green Line corridor, and rehab work in historic districts like Cathedral Hill and Summit-University. The result is a much wider mix of framing assemblies in any given calendar quarter — three- and four-story wood-frame on University Ave, Type V over Type I parking podium near the airport, and tight-tolerance retrofit carpentry in historically protected areas.

We're about 2 hours and 30 minutes from St. Paul from Long Prairie, MN. Snow load and wind requirements mirror Minneapolis — 42 psf ground snow load, 115 mph design wind speed — but St. Paul's permit and inspection process is run through DSI (Department of Safety and Inspections), and we maintain familiarity with their framing-inspection sequence. For the Selby-Dale, Frogtown, and East Side neighborhoods, we pay particular attention to lot-line setbacks, fire-rated party walls in side-by-side townhome configurations, and the egress detailing that DSI flags most often.

Why Choose Us For Your St. Paul Project

01

DSI permitting fluency

Our project leads understand St. Paul DSI's inspection sequence — rough framing, shear, and final structural — and how to schedule trades around it. That keeps GC schedules from slipping at the framing-to-MEP handoff.

02

Mixed-assembly versatility

St. Paul projects range from Type V wood frame to retrofit interior carpentry. Edge Enterprise crews handle both — same superintendent, same standards, no scope-handoff between specialty subs.

03

Historic-district sensitivity

When we frame inside St. Paul's HPC-regulated districts, we work with the GC to preserve original sheathing, joist patterns, or trim profiles wherever the spec requires — and document each variance the way the heritage commission expects to see it.
